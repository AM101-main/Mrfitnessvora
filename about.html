About us

Fitness advice, minus the guesswork about who you are

mrfitnessvora was started on a simple frustration: most fitness advice is written for an average person who doesn't exist. Real people have jobs, joints, kids, night shifts and food they actually enjoy. Counselling starts there.

The story

Why "counselling" and not "coaching"

Mr. Vora spent years handing out training plans before noticing the pattern: the plans weren't the problem. People rarely failed from a lack of information — they failed because the plan never accounted for their actual week.

So the format changed. Every engagement now opens with a real conversation — about schedule, stress, injury history, food habits and what's actually realistic — before a single set or meal gets prescribed. That conversation is the product as much as the programme is.

The philosophy

Three things every session is built on

01

Your life comes first, the plan comes second

A programme that ignores your schedule is a programme you'll quit by week three.

02

Progress is tracked in habits, not just numbers

The scale matters less than whether the new habit is still there in six months.

03

Adjust weekly, don't wait for it to break

Every check-in is a chance to change course before a bad week becomes a bad quarter.

How a client moves through it

The same four stages, every time

The specifics of your plan are personal. The structure that gets you there isn't — it's the same reliable path for everyone.

Assess A full picture of your history and constraints
Align A first plan built around your real week
Advance Weekly sessions, small honest adjustments
Sustain Handover to habits that don't need supervision
